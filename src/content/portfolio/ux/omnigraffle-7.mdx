## Overview

OmniGraffle is a diagramming and vector illustration application for macOS and iOS. Version 7 focused on making everyday work more efficient, with artboards, better editing tools, customizable shortcuts, and a new onboarding experience.

It was a substantial update to The Omni Group’s best-selling application. We needed to give professional users more flexibility while preserving the familiarity of a tool they relied on.

## My role

I led research and design for the release, managing a team of UX designers, UI designers, and researchers.

I worked closely with the product manager, engineering, and QA to develop and refine the experience. I also partnered with marketing and support to prepare for the release, and with the CEO and product manager to finalize priorities.

## The challenge

Professional users were running into limitations in common workflows. Organizing and exporting multiple layouts was cumbersome without artboards. Editing vector points required unnecessary tool changes, and people wanted quicker access to frequently used commands.

Artboards presented the largest challenge. They needed to fit OmniGraffle’s existing canvas and layer hierarchy, which meant working through both interaction design and changes to the underlying drawing model.

We also needed to help new users get started. Download-to-trial conversion on the Mac App Store was lower than for direct downloads from Omni’s website, prompting us to revisit how we introduced the application.

## Approach

Our UX team interviewed users, tested existing workflows, and reviewed customer requests.

We looked for places where people had to interrupt their work or repeat steps. Adding and removing vector points required switching tools. Turning a line into an editable shape meant redrawing it. Preparing export areas within layered documents took too much setup.

These findings helped us focus the release on document organization and editing efficiency.

We explored solutions with engineering early, then shared proposed designs with QA, marketing, and support. Those discussions helped us identify technical constraints, testing needs, and potential points of confusion before implementation.

## The work


### Making artboards work within OmniGraffle

Artboards would let people organize multiple layouts, define export areas, and move related content together. Their value seemed obvious to me as a designer, but we didn’t begin with a shared understanding of how they should work.

Some engineers questioned the need for them. They also had valid concerns about the changes required in OmniGraffle’s drawing model. I needed to explain the workflow more clearly and better understand the architecture myself.

<ScreenshotImage
  src={artboardLayerInterface}
  caption="OmniGraffle’s layer sidebar, showing an artboard as a layer type within the existing canvas and layer hierarchy."
  background="gold"
  alt="OmniGraffle canvas and layer sidebar, with an Artboard Layer callout pointing to the artboard layer containing a rectangle below two circle shapes."
/>

I used poster board, sketches, and a large table to demonstrate how designers use artboards to contain and arrange their work. That gave us something concrete to discuss: what belonged together, what moved, and what an export should include.

Working with engineering, we developed an approach based on the application’s existing canvas and layer hierarchy. Artboards became a layer type, with their position in the layer stack determining which content they affected. Any shape could become an artboard.

This approach gave users more control over export areas within a layered document. They could create assets at multiple resolutions or organize layouts such as application screens without rebuilding their document around a separate system.

<LightboxImage

The important design decision was to make artboards work through OmniGraffle’s existing structure. That required more explanation than a conventional artboard model, but it supported the flexibility of the application’s layered documents.

### Removing interruptions from editing

Alongside artboards, we improved smaller interactions that people repeated throughout the day.


#### Bezier point editing


Adding or removing vector points previously required switching tools. We introduced keyboard commands so people could make those changes while continuing to edit.



<ScreenshotImage
  src={bezierEditing}
  caption="OmniGraffle’s Bézier point editor, showing a selected vector shape with editable points and control handles for refining curves."
  background="plum"
  alt="OmniGraffle showing a selected Bézier shape with editable vector points and a red control handle on the canvas, alongside the layer sidebar and object inspector."
/>

We also worked with developers to refine control-handle behavior. The existing click targets made it too easy to drag a point without revealing its handles. Improving that response made detailed editing more predictable.


#### Converting lines and text into shapes


These were two of the most requested capabilities from customers. People wanted to modify strokes and letterforms directly, without recreating them as vector shapes.

<ScreenshotImage
  src={fontOutlines}
  caption="OmniGraffle’s Convert Text to Shapes command, showing a letter transformed into an editable vector outline."
  background="rust"
  alt="OmniGraffle’s Edit menu with Convert Text to Shapes selected, showing a letter A converted from text into an editable vector outline."
/>

We added conversion commands to the Edit menu. The focus was on preserving the original appearance while producing a manageable set of editable vector points.


#### Customizable keyboard shortcuts


We brought shortcut customization into OmniGraffle, where people could find and adjust shortcuts for both tools and menu commands.

<ScreenshotImage
  src={keyboardShortcuts}

Single-key shortcuts made frequently used tools easier to reach. Conflict warnings helped users see when an assignment overlapped with another command.

### Helping new users find a starting point

The onboarding work addressed three questions: What can I do with OmniGraffle? What has changed? How do I begin?

I worked with our video producer to create platform-specific videos that played when users first launched OmniGraffle, highlighting what was new on each platform. These introductions helped users discover the latest features and understand how to use them in their work.

## Results

Following the onboarding improvements, download-to-trial conversion increased by 23% on the Mac App Store and 16% for direct downloads from Omni’s website.

For the release overall, sales exceeded OmniGraffle 6 during the comparable three-month period. OmniGraffle 7 also remained among the top 10 grossing apps on the Mac App Store for seven weeks.

Feedback on artboards was particularly positive among the creative professionals we had targeted. Together with the editing improvements, they addressed workflows that had previously required extra steps or workarounds.

## Overview

This mobile-first education platform was designed for working adults pursuing an accredited bachelor’s degree. It brought course content, assessments, grades, and progress tracking into an experience students could use primarily on their phones.

The challenge was making that experience approachable while supporting the academic requirements behind it. Students needed clear paths through their coursework, and faculty needed assessment and grading behavior that reflected the program’s standards.

## My role

I led product design, defining the interaction model and developing the mobile learning and assessment flows.

My work moved from an early proof of concept into more detailed interaction design as academic requirements became clearer. I mapped assessment behavior, worked through grading and progression rules, and refined the interface using established mobile conventions.

## The challenge

The institution’s president supported the mobile-first vision. Faculty and operational stakeholders had questions about whether the format could preserve academic rigor and meet accreditation requirements.

Those questions affected the product’s behavior. Different assessment types required distinct evaluation rules. Students needed to understand their grades, track progress, and know what they could access next.

The first release also had to fit a limited timeline. Producing enough content for multiple degree programs was a substantial operational task, so decisions about product scope and program breadth needed to account for that workload.

## Approach

We began with a streamlined path from the home screen into program content. This proof of concept helped demonstrate how the learning experience could work on mobile.

As the project progressed, assessment and grading requirements revealed complexity that the initial prototype had not addressed. Accreditation discussions helped clarify those requirements and informed launch priorities.

I focused on mapping the interactions before refining their appearance. This gave us a way to examine how academic rules would affect the student experience and identify questions early.

## The work

### Connecting content, assessments, and progress

The experience needed to support a sequence of related activities: accessing course material, completing assessments, reviewing results, and understanding progress.

I worked through these as connected flows. The goal was to make each step understandable and help students recognize what they had completed and what came next.

### Designing around different assessment rules

The client requested multiple assessment types for the first release, each with its own grading logic.

I developed rough interaction models for each type before moving into visual refinement. This helped establish how students would complete an assessment and how its evaluation rules would be represented.

Working through the behavior first kept the design grounded in the academic requirements.

### Using familiar mobile interactions

Once the flows were established, I refined the interface around familiar mobile controls and patterns.

Students would bring different levels of technical confidence to the platform. Consistent navigation and clear feedback helped them focus on their coursework rather than learning unfamiliar interactions.

### Keeping the first release focused

An in-app community feature was considered during development. Delivering it well would have required substantial additional design and engineering work.

We deferred it and focused the release on content delivery, assessments, and progress tracking. This kept attention on the core experience students needed to participate in the program.

## Results

The platform entered private beta shortly after my involvement concluded.

Early student feedback highlighted navigation clarity and ease of use across different levels of technical comfort. Feedback also identified opportunities to improve font sizing, video progress indicators, offline access, and explanations of locked content.

These findings provided direction for further refinement. My involvement ended before broader launch outcomes were available.
